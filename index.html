Popular tips

Small habits that add up

Start with two sessions a week

Consistency matters more than intensity. Two short sessions a week is a real start, and you can build from there.

Warm up for five minutes

Gentle movement that raises your body temperature makes the main session feel better and easier to control.

Plan at least one easy day

Recovery is part of training. Notice how you feel, sleep well and ease off when you feel run down.

    Nutrition and wellness

    Simple eating, steady habits

    General, practical guidance for everyday eating. It is not a diet plan or medical advice. Adapt every idea to your food, budget and needs.

    Balanced eating tips

    Build your plate

    Fill half with vegetables or fruit. Add protein, whole grains and a little healthy fat such as olive oil, nuts or avocado.

    Eat a variety of foods

    Different colors and food groups cover a wider range of nutrients than a short list of favorites.

    Slow down at meals

    Sit down to eat when you can, and stop when you feel comfortably full rather than stuffed.

    Simple meal ideas

    Breakfast

    Oats with banana, plain yogurt and a handful of nuts.

    Lunch

    Chickpea and vegetable bowl with brown rice and a lemon dressing.

    Dinner

    Stir-fried tofu or chicken with broccoli, peppers and noodles or rice.

    Snack

    Apple with peanut butter, or carrot sticks with hummus.

    Hydration guidance

    Thirst is a useful guide for most healthy adults, and pale yellow urine is a simple check. Needs change with heat, activity and health, so ask a clinician if you have specific needs.

    A common starting point is about eight glasses a day. Adjust it to how you feel, and tap the glasses to track today.

    Healthy everyday habits

    • Move a little every hourShort walks or stretches add up across a day of sitting.
    • Keep a steady sleep routineRegular sleep supports energy, mood and recovery.
    • Cook more at homeYou control portions, salt and oil when you cook for yourself.
    • Read labels once in a whileCheck serving sizes and added sugar on packaged food.

    Basic nutrition education

    Protein
    Helps build and repair tissue. Found in eggs, beans, fish, dairy, tofu and meat.
    Carbohydrates
    The body's main fuel. Whole grains, fruit, vegetables and legumes also provide fiber.
    Fats
    Needed for hormones and for absorbing some vitamins. Nuts, seeds, olive oil and fish are common choices.
    Fiber
    Supports digestion and fullness. Found in vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains.
    Vitamins and minerals
    Needed in small amounts. A varied diet usually covers them, so supplements are best used only when advised.

    • LunchWhole-wheat wrap with chicken or chickpeas, salad and hummus.
    • DinnerSheet-pan salmon or beans with potatoes and green beans.

    Light and simple day

    • BreakfastBoiled eggs with whole-grain toast.
    • LunchLarge green salad with beans, seeds and an olive oil dressing.
    • DinnerVegetable soup with a whole-grain roll.
    • SnackPlain yogurt with pear and cinnamon.

    Portions depend on your appetite, body size and activity. Treat these as examples to adapt, not targets.

    About PULSEFORM

    Fitness information that is open to everyone.

    PULSEFORM exists to make useful movement and nutrition guidance easy to find, understand and act on. Good information should not sit behind a sign-up form or a wall of jargon.

    The guidance here is general and evidence-informed. It does not replace advice from a doctor, physiotherapist or registered dietitian, especially if you have an injury, a medical condition or are pregnant.
